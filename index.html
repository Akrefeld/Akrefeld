Hi — welcome to my homepage.

I am a researcher in Marketing and Associate Professor at the Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University. My research combines cognitive science and marketing to better understand consumer behaviour, managerial decision making, and behavioural interventions. I am particularly interested in uncovering the psychological mechanisms of behavioural interventions to explain what works, when, and for whom.

To that end, I aim to combine psychological theories with recent advances in machine learning and text modeling. My recent work also addresses climate risk communication and behaviour change interventions in more applied settings.

Before joining RSM in 2021, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Center for Decision Sciences at Columbia Business School, working with Eric J. Johnson. I hold a PhD in Marketing and Management from the University of Geneva and a PhD in Social Psychology from the University of Bern.

My research has been funded by an NWO Veni grant from the Dutch Research Council, a Convergence Resilient Delta seed grant and the Swiss National Science Foundation, and I have presented it in invited talks at, among others, Stanford, UC Berkeley, Yale, Princeton, Wharton and Chicago Booth. At RSM I teach Noise: Understanding Errors in Human Judgement and Prediction in the MSc Marketing, the MBA summer school and PhD courses, and I supervise master theses in Marketing & Data Science.

Research

I combine cognitive theory, computational modeling and large-scale behavioural data to understand how people search for information, form preferences and respond to behavioural interventions. Methodologically, my work spans Bayesian hierarchical modeling of cognitive processes, process tracing (eye- and mouse-tracking) and large-scale text analysis with large language models.

Consumer search & choice

Heterogeneity & generalizability

  • Measuring population heterogeneity (PNAS, 2025)

LLMs & digital twins

Whether LLM-based simulations of consumers can predict the heterogeneity of real people's preferences.

  • Training data limits the prediction of consumer heterogeneity by LLM-based digital twins (preprint, 2026)

Climate risk & sustainable behaviour

How risk communication and targeted interventions can move people to act on climate risks and live more sustainably.

SPA (Some People Around)

Some People Around is a salon-style format exploring the art of gathering, connecting and community building across the arts and sciences, which I started together with my husband, the artist Philipp Schwalb. Each of us invites people from our own networks, and we often use a panel from Aby Warburg's Mnemosyne Atlas to bring the group together.
